# Exploration #5 — Shopify App Store path

**Research date: 2026-11-10. Product: Ad Attribution Auditor. Decision: defer App Store development; re-score from 2.5/5 to 2.0/5 for the current launch.**

The Shopify App Store can support a one-off audit, but it is not a directory where we can list the existing CSV-and-PDF service unchanged. Shopify requires useful software integrated with its platform; an embedded audit workspace with order import, spend uploads, data checks, billing, job status, and report access is the defensible implementation. A relevant precedent exists: DataGap advertises a **$167 one-time tracking audit**, including downloadable reports, with **no reviews** visible. That supports the format, not demand for our offer or approval of a human-delivered audit. Fees are relatively favorable for an eligible small developer; engineering, review uncertainty, historical-order access, and acquiring the first merchants are the larger obstacles. Incrementality is already sold by measurement vendors, and our observational estimator cannot unconditionally “verify” causation. Recommend **2.0/5**, below Upwork Catalog, direct sale, and agency white-label; revisit as a later credibility and data-access channel after real-seller validation. Sources checked 2026-11-10: [Shopify app requirements](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements), [DataGap listing](https://apps.shopify.com/datagap?surface_detail=store-management-operations-analytics&surface_inter_position=1&surface_intra_position=22&surface_type=category&surface_version=redesign), [Shopify revenue share](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share), and [Northbeam pricing](https://www.northbeam.io/pricing).

## 1. Listing mechanics and gating

### Can the current manual service be listed?

**Not unchanged.** The requirements mandate an operational merchant UI, meaningful use of Shopify APIs, an embedded experience, immediate authentication on installation, and Shopify billing for app charges unless Shopify grants an exception. A listing that merely forwards the merchant to a payment page or a human service is a poor compliance proposition. There is no published minimum screen count or requirement for a continuously updating analytics dashboard. Whether a delayed, human-reviewed audit provides sufficient app functionality remains **review discretion**; Shopify reserves rejection discretion. Source, as of research date: [App Store requirements, especially functionality, platform integration, installation, and billing](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements).

**Proposed minimum functional product, not an approval guarantee:** inside admin, the merchant should be able to select the analysis period and margin, import store orders, upload ad exports, see date/currency/completeness problems, approve a clearly described audit purchase, submit the job, see its actual state, and open/download the completed report. Show the report's conclusions and caveats in the app as well as offering a PDF. Human analysis can happen behind this interface, but a static “we received your files” page with email-only fulfillment leaves substantially more review risk. This is an implementation judgment based on the rules above and the [one-off software audit precedent](https://apps.shopify.com/datagap?surface_detail=store-management-operations-analytics&surface_inter_position=1&surface_intra_position=22&surface_type=category&surface_version=redesign), not Shopify approval of this particular design.

## 3. Pricing and billing norms

The inspected cohort illustrates several patterns: monthly profit/order tiers (Lifetimely), fixed monthly plans (SegMetrics), order-based usage plus base subscriptions (Littledata), GMV-based recurring pricing (Polar), custom/external pricing (Northbeam/WorkMagic), and an explicit one-time audit (DataGap). Published trials in this cohort range from **14 to 30 days**, with sources in the corresponding rows above. This is a sample description, not a quantified Store-wide norm. A “free to install” badge must not be read as a free service.

**Mechanism:** Shopify App Pricing is the default for new public apps with supported subscription/usage models, but currently does **not** support one-time app purchases. Shopify explicitly directs that case to **Manual Pricing using the Billing API**. Existing external-billing notices are not permission for our new app to use Stripe; do not assume another vendor's exception applies. Sources, as of research date: [Billing model selection](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/billing), [App Store billing requirement](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements).

For our GIVEN **$99 / $199 / $399** offers, implement separate audit purchase choices, each creating an **`appPurchaseOneTimeCreate`** charge with its description, amount/currency and return URL. The returned confirmation URL sends the merchant to Shopify approval. Confirm the actual purchase state server-side before releasing paid work; a browser returning to the app is not sufficient evidence. Bind purchase entitlement to a specific shop and audit, prevent duplicates on retries, and restore the existing purchased report after reinstall. A repeat audit should require a new explicit purchase, not an accidental recurring subscription. Shopify's API describes one-time app services and professional-service/setup charges, so the payment primitive fits even though the app's overall approval remains separate. Sources: [One-time purchase guide](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/billing/manual-pricing/support-one-time-purchases), [current mutation reference](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/mutations/appPurchaseOneTimeCreate), accessed 2026-11-10.

### Fees and margin

As of **2026-11-10**, Shopify's standard eligible-developer schedule is **0% revenue share on the first $1,000,000 lifetime gross app revenue earned from 2025-01-01**, then **15% above it**, plus a separate **2.9% processing fee**. The threshold aggregates apps/associated developer accounts, not each new app separately. Developers with at least **$20,000,000 prior-year Store revenue** or at least **$100,000,000 gross company revenue** pay **15% on all app revenue**. Applicable taxes and country-specific regulatory operating fees can also apply. The owner's actual aggregate eligibility was not inspected. Sources: [Shopify revenue-share schedule](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share), [Partner agreement](https://www.shopify.com/partners/terms).

This excludes taxes, regional surcharges, refunds, hosting, labor, support and acquisition. It is proceeds after modeled platform fees, not business profit. Input prices are GIVEN; fee inputs are the [official Shopify schedule](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share).

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $99 | $2.87 | $96.13 | $14.85 | $81.28 |

Calculated retention is **97.1%** of price in the initial eligible band and **82.1%** at the modeled higher share. Contribution is `price × (1 − processing rate − revenue-share rate) − fulfillment − hosting/support − acquisition − refund losses`. The product's GIVEN assumed merchant gross margin is an input to its break-even iROAS analysis, **not** our service margin. Direct sale also has payment costs; without its actual processor terms, no net channel-cost advantage is claimed.

**Refund fit:** keep the GIVEN first-orders unconditional refund promise and the insufficient-data/wrong/late refund triggers. Shopify permits paid-charge refunds through the Partner Dashboard; unpaid charges require a credit instead. Processing fees are **not returned** on refunds. On a fully refunded modeled purchase, the processing amounts in the table remain a cost before labor. A future implementation must distinguish returning money from granting future app credit; the latter alone does not satisfy the product promise. Source: [Refund app charges](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/billing/billing-adjustments/refund-app-charges), as of research date. Nothing was charged or refunded during this research.

## 4. Fit analysis — the funnel problem

**Proposed flow — author design, dated 2026-11-10:** `listing → install/authorize → embedded eligibility and data intake → Shopify charge approval → audit queue → in-admin result/PDF`. The commercial outcome can survive, but the current pay-before-upload sequence should change to identify obvious data insufficiency before billing.

| Existing step (GIVEN) | App Store implementation proposal | What breaks / what survives |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page | Public listing plus optional educational landing page linking to the listing; example report, limits, eligibility, delivery terms and one-off pricing. | Value proposition survives. Listing copy must make the asynchronous, finite scope clear before installation. The existing unpublished demo is supporting explanation, not production functionality. |
| Pay | Install and Shopify authorization first; let the merchant inspect scope and data eligibility, then choose and approve the audit charge. | Generic direct checkout is replaced by Shopify app billing. Install consent and charge approval are separate decisions. Staff without billing permission may need an authorized colleague. |
| Upload Shopify orders | Request read access, retrieve the chosen period, normalize to the engine's order schema, reconcile totals, and show coverage. Offer order CSV fallback for historical-access problems, with provenance shown. | API import can eliminate the Shopify export/upload. It creates a data-access/reconciliation obligation and does not itself remove the ad uploads. |
| Upload Meta/Google spend | Retain separate campaign-by-day exports and validate them inside the app. | The merchant still has manual work. Shopify authorization is not authorization to read the merchant's independent advertising accounts. Full spend connectors are additional integrations, outside the minimal scope. |
| Processing | Show real states: awaiting files, data issue, queued, analyst review, ready, or refunded. Start the GIVEN delivery clock when complete valid inputs are accepted and state that trigger explicitly. | Human review and the GIVEN 72-hour turnaround can survive; an invented progress animation cannot substitute for job tracking. Listing acceptance of this delay remains discretionary. |
| Report | Display summary, classification, uncertainty, margin assumption and warnings inside admin; provide secured PDF access and optional completion email. | A finite artifact survives. Admin report access is more defensible than email-only delivery; email is an additional notification/delivery option. Uninstall and deletion must have a defined effect on access. |

Technical basis for the proposed install/billing flow: [Shopify scaffold authentication](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/authentication-authorization/cli-app-authentication), [one-time purchase guide](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/billing/manual-pricing/support-one-time-purchases), and [one-time purchase object and staff authorization](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/AppPurchaseOneTime), accessed 2026-11-10.

### Can Shopify supply the equivalent input data?

**Orders: broadly yes, subject to access and semantic reconciliation.** GraphQL order records expose timestamps, amounts/currencies, cancellations, discounts, taxes, shipping and refund-related information. Build a deterministic mapping to the audit's chosen revenue definition; do not blindly treat total order value as the same as the CSV revenue metric. Specify store timezone, reporting currency, test-order exclusion, refund timing, and date boundaries. Reconcile the imported daily totals against the seller's corresponding Shopify export/report during pilots. The source supports field availability, not that our mapping already works: [Order API reference](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/admin-graphql/latest/objects/Order), as of research date.

**History is a real gate:** unapproved API access is limited to the default **60-day** order window. Longer analysis periods require the separately approved scope described in section 1 or an explicit CSV fallback. Do not silently truncate the audit or present the shorter window as the requested full history. Source: [Shopify order permissions](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/usage/access-scopes), accessed 2026-11-10. The engine's required window should be established by data sufficiency/MDE, not a convenient API limit.

**Ad spend: the two uploads survive in the minimum product.** We did not verify a Shopify endpoint granting arbitrary access to the seller's full Meta/Google campaign-by-day spend and platform-reported metrics. Google's official documentation separately requires Google account authorization and production API access; Shopify installation does not grant it. Meta documentation fetches were rate-limited, so exact current Meta permissions/review requirements are **UNVERIFIED** and not specified here. Sources: [Google Ads authorization](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/oauth/overview), [current Google API access policy](https://developers.google.com/google-ads/api/docs/api-policy/access-levels), [Meta Insights documentation attempted](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/insights/), accessed 2026-11-10.

There is a narrower Shopify exception: an official **2026-08-10** announcement exposes **Shop Campaigns** performance through ShopifyQL. It does not establish access to all independent Meta/Google campaigns and therefore does not replace our required exports. Source: [Shop Campaigns analytics announcement](https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/see-shop-campaigns-performance-in-your-analytics-tools).

**Expectation mismatch — inference from the surveyed listings, not merchant interview evidence:** live tracking, connected channels and dashboards recur throughout the cohort. A seller who installs our app could reasonably expect automatic spend sync or an immediate answer. Counter that before purchase with explicit copy: one audit, manual ad exports, human review, no automatic ongoing measurement, and possible inconclusive results. Will merchants accept that tradeoff? **UNVERIFIED until pilots.** API-connected orders reduce intake friction; they do not fix endogeneity, omitted demand controls, adstock, or causal identification.

A CSV-first embedded variant might reduce the estimate to approximately **15–25 engineering days** by removing most automated order ingestion, **but acceptance is especially uncertain** if Shopify API usage is superficial. This is a lower-priority experiment, not a recommended way to evade meaningful integration. Replacing order ingestion with uploads still requires auth, billing, storage, tenant isolation, privacy, status and report delivery. The [platform API requirement](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/shopify-app-store/app-store-requirements) is the reason that reducing code can increase review risk.

Largest unknowns, in order of decision impact:

1. Whether the asynchronous human-reviewed workflow is accepted as a useful app; no public pre-approval of our exact model exists.
2. Historical-order and protected-data access; incorrect revenue mapping or truncated history can invalidate the report even when installation succeeds.
3. Whether pilot data supports an actionable result at all. More polished intake cannot repair the GIVEN estimator limitations.
4. Review queue/remediation duration and newly changing platform controls. The estimate contains basic remediation, not an unlimited rejection/rebuild cycle.
5. Whether users will finish the remaining ad-export steps and pay for a delayed, potentially inconclusive diagnostic.

Hosting, storage, email and ongoing support costs are **not priced**: no public deployment architecture or measured workload establishes them yet. Do not manufacture a monthly infrastructure budget. Even a one-off product retains maintenance obligations after its last report sale; Shopify performs [ongoing quality checks](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/app-store-review/app-quality-checks).

## 6. Discovery reality check

**A listing is distribution infrastructure, not an assured audience.** Shopify documents discovery through Store browsing/search, admin recommendations, and Sidekick, all drawing on public listing information. Its marketing guidance explicitly recommends external traffic, listing optimization, reviews, and optional Store ads. It says outside traffic can help ranking. Sources, as of research date: [About the App Store](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/app-store-review), [Shopify marketing guidance](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/marketing).

Published levers and their limits:

| Lever | Verified statement | Implication for a new listing |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance and listing content | Shopify recommends relevant natural-language keywords, complete fields, and measuring listing changes; keyword stuffing can reduce discoverability. [Marketing guidance](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/marketing) | Use language about ad-spend audits, reporting, and ROAS; search demand for our exact one-off proposition is **UNVERIFIED**. No claim about ranking weights or current keyword difficulty. |
| Reviews | Shopify says useful positive reviews help search/category position. Ratings weight recency, usefulness and trustworthiness, rather than being a simple mean. [Review guidance](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/marketing/manage-app-reviews) | Starting without reviews loses a trust signal, but no published universal review-count threshold for organic ranking was found. Review count is not proof of adoption. |
| Built for Shopify | Requires **50 net installs from active shops on paid plans**, at least **5 reviews**, and a minimum recent-rating threshold, alongside technical requirements. The retrieved page does not specify a numeric rating cutoff. [BFS requirements](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/built-for-shopify/requirements) | These are badge prerequisites, not requirements to launch a first app or guarantees of search placement. A finite-use report app may struggle to retain active installations and usage. |
| BFS exposure | Shopify documents ranking boosts and eligibility for promotional/admin surfaces, with personalized placement not guaranteed. [BFS program](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/built-for-shopify) | Later credibility benefit; a zero-traction launch cannot assume access to it. |
| Categories and features | A primary tag is required; Shopify reviews categorization. [Category documentation](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/app-store-review/app-listing-categories) | Propose Analytics with marketing-attribution/custom-report features that actually exist; Ads only if justified. It is an audit/reporting utility, not a sales channel. Final placement is review-controlled. |
| Advertising | Shopify offers ads in search, category/subcategory and homepage placements. [Store overview](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/app-store-review) | Paid exposure exists, but this research establishes no required spend, CPC, conversion rate, or payback. No campaign was purchased. |

**Is it pay-to-play or review-gated?** Neither is established as a hard rule. Official documentation supports meaningful review/relevance advantages and optional paid discovery, not “you must buy ads” or “you need a particular review count to rank.” SegMetrics and DataGap visibly have no reviews, while Northbeam has few; these listings demonstrate availability, not meaningful organic acquisition. Evidence is insufficient to forecast first-month installs or CAC. The realistic planning assumption is **unproven organic demand and a need to bring initial qualified users ourselves**, not guaranteed free traffic.

**Proposed first-install path — no outreach executed:** complete the GIVEN free-pilot gate through owner-approved channels first; learn whether sellers can supply the data and value the report. If an app later becomes justified, invite willing validated users to install the actual utility, measure listing views → installs → complete uploads → approved purchases → delivered usable findings → refunds, and improve the biggest observed drop-off. Ask users for honest reviews after a real experience without incentives or positivity filtering. Shopify permits reviews from installed merchants and within **45 days after uninstall**; fake/incentivized manipulation is prohibited. Source: [Review eligibility and policy](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/marketing/manage-app-reviews), as of research date.

An existing direct or agency acquisition motion would therefore seed the app; the app does not eliminate that work. A one-off engagement also provides fewer recurring occasions to bring merchants back into admin. Those are business-model inferences, not measured Store conversion effects.
